# Construction and buildings

Settlements build from authored blueprints and can certify player-built structures and natural work zones.

See [jobs](jobs.md), [menus](menus.md), [breeding](breeding.md), and the [settlement architecture](../architecture/settlements.md).

Runtime billing uses explicit project handles or the joint active bill, and carry returns address a builder UUID. Queue-fixture stamping and plain-text failure diagnostics are test-only, not alternate runtime construction paths.

## Blueprint files

Krasnokamen owns the JSON templates in `crates/mc-core/assets/blueprints/`. The embedded catalog has 21 blueprints, one per building kind (`id` is the `/settlement build` argument):

| Category | Blueprints | Levels |
|---|---|---|
| Housing | `house` | 1–3 |
| Storage | `storage` | 1–2 |
| Production | `farm`, `mason` (the Builder hut), `lumber_camp`, `mine` | 1–2 |
| Production, owner-ordered workshops | `armorer`, `butcher`, `cartographer`, `cleric`, `fisherman`, `fletcher`, `leatherworker`, `librarian`, `toolsmith`, `weaponsmith` | 1–2 |
| Production, shepherd | `shepherd` | 1 |
| Military | `barracks` | 1–3 |
| Fortification | `wall`, `watch_tower`, `gate` | 1–3 |

Each workshop is built around its profession's vanilla job-site block (blast furnace, smoker, cartography table, brewing stand, barrel, fletching table, cauldron, lectern, smithing table, grindstone), one more per level, so a resident of that profession claims it like any certified workshop. A workshop's bill includes that block, which workers do not produce (iron, flint, books, a blaze rod): stock the delivered item or its ingredients first. The gate opens one more door per level between four solid pillars; wall, tower and barracks level 3 meet the level-3 certificate dimensions below (13 × 6 wall, 10-high tower, 6 beds). A geometric certificate can reach level 3 even when its blueprint has no corresponding upgrade.

Each file has an `id`, a building `kind`, a character-to-block-state `palette`, and `levels`. Each level contains `cert`, the certification box dimensions `[x, y, z]`; `entrance`, an `[x, y, z]` offset; and bottom-up `layers`, each made of Z rows of X characters. A dot is air. Palette states can include properties, for example `loom[facing=north]`. Each level is a complete picture, not an upgrade patch: the loader derives placements, removals, materials and rotations from it. A level must keep every container of the previous level (chest, barrel, furnace and other inventories) as the same block state; the loader refuses a level that replaces or removes one, because Builders never write over a stocked container and such an upgrade could not finish. The maximum placed dimensions are 13 × 11 × 11 blocks: 13 is the level-3 wall's length, which runs along its road, and a lot is at most 11 deep across the front a building turns to the road.

The origin is the minimum corner of the rotated **maximum footprint across all levels**. Its Y is the ground-surface block, replaced by the floor layer. Certification bounds rotate in the same frame and may be smaller than the placed footprint. Upgrades retain the origin, prepared foundation and rotation. The farm's certificate includes its field as well as its shed.

For authoring, `tools/blueprints.py check` checks structure, `tools/blueprints.py materials [id ...]` estimates material totals, and `tools/blueprints.py preview [id ...]` writes PNG previews. Material estimates are not runtime funding proofs: recipes may have fractional estimates even though real crafting consumes whole batches. The Rust blueprint tests check certification of the levels; the Python check is not that gate.

## Planning and streets

Keep the anchor, storage and worksite chunks loaded. Construction uses real items in certified storage and the generated village's bootstrap container, not player inventories. A player-founded settlement has no free stock; certify storage and stock it.

The planner selects one active settlement every 20 ticks. It considers missing kinds in this order: storage; houses when certified house capacity is below population; farm; lumber camp; mine; Builder hut (`mason`); barracks from 6 residents; tower from 8. Natural mine and lumber zones do not count as built blueprint kinds. The shepherd's hut and the ten profession workshops are never started autonomously; the owner orders them, and so are single wall sections and gates (`/settlement build wall|gate`): from 10 residents the planner instead walls the settlement in itself, see [perimeter, gates and well](#perimeter-gates-and-well). Funded upgrades are considered for eligible blueprint-built certificates; workshops requiring iron or flint are not on this automatic list. A need without available stock or a capable local source does not conjure materials or have to block another funded kind.

**Receiving capacity (owner D54).** When fewer than one quarter of the physical
deposit slots are empty, another storehouse takes priority over other autonomous
construction. The existing owner-order precedence is unchanged. At most one
storehouse may be active or pending; there is no store-count cap beyond the
ordinary territory and legal lots. Capacity uses the executor's own deduplicated
source images, including its physical-container limit, not a second chest index.
The first cultural store remains unchanged; further Spanish capacity uses the
ordinary wood/cobblestone `storage` warehouse, not another calcite civic plaza.
Emergency priority and the receiving-capacity fence apply after a storehouse is
completed; a missing first civic store keeps its ordinary admission and fallback
to other funded work.

An additional storehouse's bill is ordered by resource name before the existing
species and joint-reservation funding proof, rather than by protocol item id.
Consuming an owed small stack can then make real room for a later recipe output;
no recipe or capacity check is waived. This funding projection never supplies
slots to the preparation fence, which still reads the current physical images.
The selected haul is ordered by its concrete resource names as well, so a small
owed stack can leave the bank before a recipe needs its slot. The existing
one-pass packing, joint reservations and atomic inventory commit are unchanged.
Hauling is not moved ahead of terrain clearing, and no future loan is counted as
receiving capacity.

A pressured storehouse is refused **inside the normal candidate sweep** if its
early drops cannot be conservatively received. The bound reserves an empty slot
per possible terrain/plant cut and two per retired crop; it deliberately does not
count partial-stack merge room or future Builder fetches. Terrain heights also
bound cuts above the authored roof. Any tree on the lot or a possible walking
corridor refuses that candidate: preparation may fell its whole registered tree
outside the footprint, with random leaf loot, before Fill. Tree-column answers
and the construction walk are reused for the pass; this fence never materializes
preparation or reruns re-layout per candidate. The sweep can therefore choose a
clean alternative instead of repeatedly restarting at an unsafe cheapest lot.
At fully occupied storage, admission needs a genuinely cut-free preparation;
later Fill is never subtracted to make the first Cut appear payable. Existing
transactional no-loss/no-duplication behavior remains unchanged.

An unfunded priority bill does not stop the search for funded buildings or upgrades. Its staffed extraction worker is protected from Builder recruitment for autonomous work, leaving another resident free to build; explicit owner projects retain their active-bill gathering rules. Optional street projects still wait for that priority bill.

New buildings without a legal lot do not suppress funded upgrades: after the bounded new-site search, the planner can upgrade an existing blueprint-built house inside its already reserved maximum footprint. Both `house` and `spanish_house` intentionally provide one, two and three beds at levels 1, 2 and 3; the level-one house is not an eight-resident dormitory. Growth needs further houses or upgrades and a physically suitable spare bed.

For a Spanish owner cohort, a missing `spanish_church` is an additional autonomous Cleric workshop candidate after the ordinary missing kinds, including the tower at eight residents. An unordered tower before a church follows this priority; funding and legal sites can skip an earlier candidate.

Construction admits up to **eight active projects** and retains up to **eight pending owner orders**. Active work keeps its slot even before its first cell; later requests never replace or preempt it. Pending orders are considered in submission order, but an unfunded or site-blocked order permits a later funded owner order to use a free slot. Any pending order fences unrelated autonomous admission, including houses. When the queue is empty, the planner may choose a fully fundable culture-appropriate house if live paired, roofed bed capacity is at or below population and a site is available. This need uses the allocator's completed physical POI index, not certificate levels.

A pending unfunded order still allows its own furnace prerequisite, paid through ordinary construction when that order's iron or shears demand has raw iron, fuel and furnace materials. It does not allow a furnace for unrelated autonomous demand. An already prepared founding expedition can also hand over a fully built outpost or cancel after losing its eligible household; admitting an expedition or starting another outpost structure waits for the pending order.

The Spain trial driver skips cultural blueprints already completed or active in
the observed parent settlement before issuing its next order. In particular,
it does not order a second autonomous fortress and leave an unfunded duplicate
fencing all later autonomous housing.
The population-16 expansion gift is one bounded manifest, not a new grant on
each census. It tops up observed existing stacks before using empty slots and
delivers only its remaining units at later scheduled censuses as storage clears.
`run.json` records that remainder explicitly; initial and daughter fixtures
still require their complete delivery before observation continues.
Long Spain phase profiles use disk output (omit `--scratch`). Raw output remains
complete, while command readers exclude millisecond, microsecond and item-census
diagnostics. The settlement tail measures Overworld laps only: a search logs the
current tick, and its completed native phase header names the following tick.

The native `civilization-seed-qa` census uses `minecraft_list_entities` cursor pages of 16 across the original 128-block radius, without reducing or dropping entity kinds. Native type filtering is used only for the targeted newborn refresh; birth proof still requires that same UUID's received baby metadata, targeted screenshot and final checkpoint audit. This chooses native cursor pagination rather than replacing the client census with server counts: the original client-visible coverage remains intact while each MCP reply stays below its transport byte limit. Use a pinned client exporting these optional arguments through `mcp-schema`; the Loader schema remains its own actual Java catalog.

Each native paginated census captures its complete matching received rows on the first page. Later pages retain that membership and metadata despite incoming packets or player motion; continuation tokens cannot be replayed, reused after a new census, or used with another context/query. The harness checks the same total on every page and never substitutes a partial baseline.

Non-house building milestone captures keep the original inside-building look target but navigate to one supported exterior standpoint selected deterministically from received perimeter geometry, using the same dry-cell walkability check as the village approach. A bounding-volume midpoint is neither a player-feet position nor a route through a closed door. Missing support fails the capture; there are no candidate retries, door mutations, teleports or relaxed ray-target checks. House captures instead approach the received entrance, use it ordinarily if closed, await both open door halves and retain the exact-bed raycast and screenshot before returning to the original observer.

The deterministic adopted-village closure runs the native root tick, including renewable growth and block publication, rather than advancing only autonomy, settlements and brains. It still requires all four harvested source types, built storage and housing, certified natural worksites and a Builder; construction evidence is published plank placement rather than an undrained pending-write buffer. Closure is deterministic at tick 72,901; the test is bounded at 80,000 ticks and never substitutes a shorter successful scenario. Instead of a wall-clock guard it checks a deterministic work budget on every tick: at most 30,000 full funding proofs and 1,500,000 recipe candidates over the run (closure uses about 20,000 and 870,000). Disabling the recipe index, dependency admission or the funding memo separately exceeds that budget. Timings belong to profiling runs, not to this test. This is fixture evidence, not server-capacity or graphical acceptance. Movement follows admitted navigation targets in this fixture; graphical movement remains the separate real-client gate.

The street plan is fixed relative to the bell: roads are three blocks wide every 28 blocks on both axes, with a one-block verge, an open 9 × 9 plaza, and two back-to-back rows of lots up to 11 blocks deep. The wall line is the outer verge of the first ring road, 30 blocks from the bell in each direction: the ring road runs inside it, every lot of the four blocks around the bell keeps its frontage, and no lot reaches the line. `/settlement layout` reports the plan, reachable and obstructed roads, paving, lamps, building access and next street work.

- A frontage must be reachable from the plaza on loaded ground, with steps no higher than one block between street columns. Ground is found within eight blocks of the bell's elevation, and beyond that band from the column next to it that the walk has already reached (one block up, three down: what a villager walks), so a hillside village's streets climb. The one-block step from road to verge to a lot's floor is replaced by that same walkable step (up one, down three), so a lot's door stays reachable (owner, 2026-11-17). A funded building may include natural-forest clearing on its lot and walking corridor; admission itself changes no blocks. Water, lava, steep terrain and unrelated obstructions still cut the network off.
- A building's entrance faces its road. The planner seeks a free, fundable lot. Autonomous passes examine up to 64 candidates and resume from their cursor; a new owner request is queued at once (`Queued <blueprint>: waiting for a site`, or the funding reason) and its command examines no lot. A lot is admitted only after its whole cursor sweep has been examined, and then the **cheapest** one, measured by the number of cut, fill and liquid cells it really needs. Equal work goes first to the lot with fewer lava cells, then fewer water cells, then to the lot whose origin is nearest the settlement's anchor (the bell), then to the lot's own block position and rotation. Both depend on the lot alone, so a restart in the middle of a sweep cannot change the winner, and a village on flat ground grows from its bell rather than from the edge of its loaded plan. The sweep holds the project slot only while it already holds a legal candidate: an unfinished sweep with nothing legal yet leaves the slot to upgrades, meadow, fortification and street work. A sweep whose stored ranking no longer matches the re-checked cost restarts instead of admitting the stale winner. An unfinished sweep admits nothing, and only the winner is materialized (no per-candidate planning pass).
- **One site budget for the whole server (owner D42).** A tick examines at most two 64-candidate sweep slices (128 lots), all settlements, players and lumberjacks together: the tick's planner pass takes its slices first, then one *order turn* gives a slice to the next settlement (in settlement-id order, round robin) whose funded order waits — a new building's site search, or an upgrade's admission, which searches no site but takes a slice too — and inside a settlement to the next new building after the one searched last. An owner order, upgrade included, is only checked and queued by its command (`Queued <blueprint>: waiting for a site` / `Queued <blueprint> L<n>: ready`, or the funding reason); nothing is admitted or withdrawn in the command's tick. Many simultaneous orders therefore cost no more per tick than one planner pass; each starts (`Started <blueprint> construction.`) on its turn. A lumberjack's replanting proof (crown keeps building access and a house lot, below) is site search too: each village proof takes a slice of what the tick has left — its street walks and at most that slice's lot checks; house blueprints of one footprint share their lot checks. Per house blueprint the proof first re-checks the village's last free lot found (one check): if the walk with the new crown keeps it, nothing more is needed. Otherwise it checks, in this proof, every lot the crown would take (a lot whose door, verge or footprint the crown covers, or whose frontage the crowned walk loses): if none is free now, the crown takes nothing and is planted, so a full village needs no scan. Only a crown that takes a free lot scans its crowned walk for another, with a fair share of the slice; the crown's next proof continues that scan, which plants when it finds one (the village's next hint) and refuses when the walk has none. No proof plants on an answer older than itself. A proof without a slice, or while its scan runs, does not plant yet: the lumberjack keeps the stump and asks again on its next brain run; a waiting crown's scan outlives its last proof by 64 ticks, longer than the longest villager brain period. The turn, the hints and the scans are runtime state: after a restart a queued order waits for its settlement's planner pass and the scans start again.
- The site's terrain window is bounded by what a builder at the lot's perimeter reaches (placement reach 12 with a two-block margin). Support is discovered per column in an eight-block band around the door-step floor; a support deeper than that is `SurfaceUnavailable`, never a shaft the executor would stall on. Inside the territory a fill column deeper than four blocks is levelled when every one of its cells lies inside the territory sphere (owner, 2026-11-17: the builder levels, digs or fills); otherwise, and outside the territory (a founding site), no single fill column is deeper than four blocks below the floor (deeper is refused as `HeightVariance`, a retaining wall rather than a foundation). Gaps up to the floor are filled with real material bought from storage, and natural terrain inside the authored level may be cut. **Open gap against owner decision D7 (Main, 2026-11-19; owner decision pending).** D7 says terrain is not a refusal reason inside the settlement. The code still refuses a fill deeper than the eight-block band around the floor (`SurfaceUnavailable`) and a hillside above the authored roof (`CutBeyondEnvelope`), because a builder at the lot's perimeter cannot reach or complete that work (reviews of the levelling slice); closing the gap needs a builder reach capability, not a planner change. Everywhere, any fluid in the one-cell shell around the work volume (`LavaShell`, lava or water) refuses the site. Unloaded or unsuitable sites and protected paths, farmland, containers and other obstructions are not cleared to prepare a site. Each cleared or filled cell is work and each fill bills one real filler block. Admission is decided on the authored blueprint bill: a foundation shortfall is the active project's own wait (`collecting_material`), served by the existing gathering, crafting and carrying rules, and never a reason to skip the lot.
- **Water and lava below the authored floor are prepared as fill cells** (owner D10/D21). Levelling inside the territory reaches these paths more often (a closed bowl deeper than four blocks under a dry floor is no longer refused by the depth limit first); the liquid refusals themselves are unchanged. The fill is at most eight rows deep and the shipped catalogue's largest footprint is 121 columns, so a shipped lot stays under the 1024-cell liquid bound (968); a blueprint the validator accepts at 13 × 11 could exceed it and would be refused as `LiquidBeyondBound`, a safe named refusal. A fluid fill cell is one ordinary fill: one real filler block from storage, billed and debited exactly like a dry fill, and it is counted once — as a liquid cell, never also as a dry fill. Such a lot is admitted only as a **closed bowl**: the footprint's one-cell ring must hold fluid back (not air, not replaceable, not a fluid and not a tree, which this same plan fells) from the deepest discovered support up to the highest fluid cell, and the interior under every column's own support must be solid ground; otherwise it is `LiquidOpenShell`. Water and lava in the same work volume refuse as `MixedLiquids` (the engine would harden lava into stone or obsidian outside the bill); with lava, a flammable ring cell refuses as `CombustibleShell` because fire replaces it. A waterlogged block or bubble column in the work volume refuses as `LiquidInWork` (a source no fill can cut off), a fluid at an authored floor row refuses the same way, and outside the territory a player-attributed cell — fluid included — refuses as `ProtectedObstruction`. An actual fluid block on an unfloored floor row is admitted and becomes exactly one paid fill cell. Every plan puts fluid sources first, top-down inside their column, and the rest of the fill keeps its order; the cost counts cut, dry fill and liquid cells disjointly and ranks equal work by fewer lava cells, then fewer water cells. A lot whose own door or verge column holds fluid is still refused, now by name `LiquidAccess`. **Execution (wired with the executor).** A fill cell that holds a real fluid *source* at preparation runs in its own leading `liquid` portion before any air cut, so a cut beside lava never waits on a supply that would be filled later; the cell belongs to exactly one stage, so the bill is unchanged. Before any debit, the write gate refuses a **flowing or falling lava** cell with `lava_draining` — the cell is never written, and the gate wakes that flow's orphaned fluid tick once (a queued tick around the cell is the wake, so a repeated wait adds no entry); an air write whose six neighbours include lava waits as `lava_neighbour`; water is written immediately. A preparation stand beside lava is vetoed: the cell's support, feet and head rows and their four horizontal neighbours must all be lava-free. The certified fluid envelope includes the one-cell x/z ring and the discovered supports. Every advance re-proves that physical envelope before debit: an opened ring, missing support, unsafe remaining supply or (outside the territory) a player-attributed block returns `Blocked` without storing a stale pause or changing stock, already-paid cells or the cursor. Explicit owner-paused work re-proves the same recorded envelope again during recovery, including paid cells, and a refusal keeps that explicit pause. A wait is named from the plan entry's state, so a cut beside lava reports `lava_neighbour`, and the wake for a lava wait runs once per waiting neighbourhood: the fluid queue keeps a position index, so an outstanding tick around the cell is found without walking the queue.
- A cut credits the mined drop of its own block (one shared `villager::mining` mapping: stone to cobblestone, grass block to dirt, ore to its raw item) to real storage in the same transaction as the debit, before the block changes. Full storage waits without editing the cell or losing the material. Ores are not `excavatable`, so this slice makes no ore-credit promise: ore inside the authored level is refused (`CutBeyondEnvelope`) rather than paid out. Outside the settlement's territory a cut cell that a player touches stops the project as `Blocked`, and so does a cut cell a fluid reaches anywhere; that stop is indefinite until the owner cancels or clears the obstruction, exactly like a protected tree. The top-down order of the clear cells is a property of the plan manifest (proven by the manifest test), not a promise about falling-block physics. Outside the territory a player-attributed cell in the work volume reports `ProtectedObstruction` at survey and refuses the debit again; inside it the debit cuts and credits it. Cuts run top-down, so surviving terrain above never loses its support and a standing builder steps down at most one block per work unit.
- Fill takes the material settlement storage actually holds, most preferred first: dirt, coarse dirt, rooted dirt, podzol, cobblestone, stone. The choice happens **after** the authored bill and its whole crafting chain are reserved — a wall of stone bricks consumes stone, and its stairs more bricks, so the fill never spends what the building's own crafters need — and only the remainder is spent, from the top layer down, so the visible floor takes the preferred material. A lot whose fill is short of dirt is therefore not refused for a material it does not need; falling blocks (sand, gravel) are never fill.
- A certified natural work zone is terrain, not a building: it keeps only its own columns and reserves no one-block building setback. It refuses when the lot's whole preparation volume — the authored footprint **and the fill below it down to the lowest discovered ground** — meets the zone's bounds, so a house that would sit inside a grove or above a mine's shafts is still refused.
- When no building or upgrade starts, Builders can pave reachable road stretches serving standing buildings: natural soil becomes dirt path and replaceable plants above are cleared without a material charge. Lamps on served stretches use two fences and a torch from storage, at the planned positions every nine blocks. Stone and sand remain as they are. Once the streets owe nothing, a settlement of 10 or more residents starts [perimeter, gates and well](#perimeter-gates-and-well) work.
- Completed free paving does not reserve a street against later free paving or lamps: newly reachable soil in that stretch can still be paved. Active work, standing buildings and paid street features remain protected by the canonical structure ledger.
- The first Farm's field costs 12 wheat seeds, and only a farm produces them. While a settlement has no Farmer workshop and fewer seeds in storage than the Farm needs, the same free Builder time goes first to a **meadow clearing**, even while a priority material worker is out (that worker is not recruited for it): a 16 × 16 window within 48 blocks of the bell holding at least 8 seed-bearing plants (short grass, tall grass, fern or large fern), nearest window first, outside every building and container. The clearing costs nothing, and each plant drops its vanilla loot into real storage (a wheat seed one time in eight), so a fresh town gathers the seeds itself; a sparse savanna needs several clearings. Street work follows when no window qualifies or the stock suffices.

**Village property inside the territory (owner D27 p.4, 2026-11-18, [decision](OWNER_DECISIONS.md#village-property-inside-the-territory--owner-d27-p4-2026-11-18)):** everything built on a settlement's territory belongs to the settlement. The territory is the record's sphere (`SettlementRecord::contains`: within `radius` of the anchor), decided per modified cell, and only the working settlement's own territory counts. For a cell inside it, the site survey, the plan, a lot's ground discovery, builder footing, upgrade forest clearing, the stall watch, a water or lava fill cell and a liquid plan's resume re-proof read no block history, player attribution, persistent-leaf mark, live-player veto or generator evidence: a player's log, leaves, sapling, excavatable terrain or fluid is cleared or filled like natural ground, with its vanilla drop credited to storage. A street column's ground and headroom read no player history inside the territory either, so a player's log on or over a street column is the village's forest; an unattributed tree there still needs this world's generator evidence, which a cold town's street walk requests. Fields and village paths on a needed lot are the re-lay's own work (below), which reads no history inside the territory either. The settlement's own registered lumberjack plantings keep their protection, a container or other block-entity cell keeps refusing the lot, and the liquid rules, setbacks and the terrain window are unchanged. A daughter's founding site lies outside its parent's territory, and so do corridor, crown or fill cells beyond the sphere: there every protection in the paragraphs below stays.

Outside the territory, natural forest must match deterministic regeneration by this world's generator, with no retained player edit at that block; imported Anvil baselines and baselines without generator provenance are protected. Natural leaves may differ from regeneration in their evolving `distance` property. Regeneration runs off-tick and caches only tree evidence within a 64 MiB payload budget. Its worker stops with its world: closing the world waits for at most the chunk being generated, then releases the world's store. Lumberjack-planted sapling positions are persisted, protecting their trunks and nearby crowns after restart; vanished trees are retired when their trunk column is loaded. With block history disabled, forest within 32 horizontal blocks of a live player is additionally protected. An identical player replacement at its original natural coordinate after `/co` history expires is indistinguishable from nature and may be felled into settlement storage, with its items conserved.

New lumberjack plantings must keep their protected crown halo outside the executor's held site and access-corridor reservations. **D7/D10 needed-lot clearing:** a paid new building may clear an exact registered sapling or fell a registered grown tree needed by its lot, including its connected trunk and attached nonpersistent crown outside the footprint. Only non-stripped tree logs join the owned component; stripped logs, wood blocks, stems and hyphae remain foreign. Connectivity includes vanilla acacia's diagonal upward steps, within the existing four-block horizontal radius and 64-block height bound. A log neighbour beyond that envelope makes the entire component ambiguous, rather than truncating it. A second trunk column standing on soil or on a solid support other than tree logs or leaves also makes the component ambiguous. Foreign logs protect leaves within six face-adjacent steps; missing neighbourhood evidence keeps the tree. One immutable selection scan shares each relevant root's proof across all candidates and materialization, then drops it. Its box ends at the connected log top plus twelve blocks, accounting for both owned-leaf reach and foreign-log support. A nonoverlapping registered halo never triggers a proof; there is no cross-tick cache or executor-tick volume scan.

Vanilla and native player placement set leaves `persistent=true`; distance updates and decay do not remove that mark. Outside the territory, persistent leaves and retained player edits always veto clearing; with history disabled, a live player within 32 horizontal blocks also vetoes every permission. Unlogged operator-created `persistent=false` leaves attached to the registered tree may also be cleared. A historyless player log touching that tree without a distinct soil-root column is likewise indistinguishable from its trunk and may be felled, with conserved drops; this is the owner's accepted residual, not proof of player authorship. Other cells outside the territory still require positive deterministic natural-baseline evidence.

A registered off-lot sapling whose future halo overlaps the site refuses admission as `PlantingProvenance` until grown; an already-grown crown is checked per cell. Ambiguous grown foliage reports `AmbiguousFoliage`, while persistent or player-touched cells outside the territory report `ProtectedObstruction`. **An upgrade clears natural forest on its own new cells (owner D18, 2026-11-17):** every work-diff cell that currently holds a tree or sapling goes through the same clearance fence as a new site, and a registered root touched there is felled as a whole tree with conserved drops. Outside the territory, attribution, persistent leaves and the disabled-history live-player veto precede any authored-state exemption, and unknown or imported generator evidence still refuses; a protected halo refuses everywhere. An upgrade replaces cells matching the building's own prior authored state, and outside the territory an identical player replacement there is protected only while its history is retained.

**Touching registered groves (N5):** a log of another non-ambiguous registered component stops being foreign evidence, so leaves between two registered trunks are certified by both trees. A tree whose own log stands within six blocks of a certified lot, corridor or upgrade cell is marked needed and felled whole; a crown leaf that an *unneeded* neighbouring tree still supports is not cleared, so no neighbour is left mutilated. Needed trees are kept in a sorted set, marked only from certified work cells (no transitive expansion of the grove), and their whole-tree cells stay inside the tree's own `root ± 4` crown box and connected logs. Player attribution wins: a retained player log anywhere in a component, the root included, makes that component ambiguous and keeps the whole tree. Any tree log outside the root's column — a level lateral step, a treehouse, a beam — is treated as a player structure attached to the trunk and keeps the whole tree, with or without retained history; a sideways-grown vanilla tree is cleared through its worldgen evidence instead, per cell. Limbs that step both horizontal axes in one move stay foreign: a sapling of this server grows a straight trunk (`crates/mc-world/src/plant_rules_26_1_2.rs`) and world generation refuses `fancy_trunk_placer` (`crates/mc-worldgen/src/vanilla_features_tests.rs`), so no felled tree can carry one, and joining diagonal neighbours would instead merge two separate trunks that each stand on soil. Mixed groves of a registered and a natural tree still refuse on the natural one.

**Upgrade placement fence (D7 §2.4, owner D27).** Before a paid upgrade is admitted, every planned placement cell must hold either air — already air, or a cell the same plan empties earlier and therefore places onto air — or the block the previous build last committed at that position, compared by **raw block identity**. The evidence is the whole executed chain of that settlement's committed records for the same blueprint and site, oldest first with the last write per position winning, because a level's record holds only its diff from the level below and the newest record alone would lose cells it never touched. The chain is ordered by each record's creation revision (the one in its structure handle), not its last-change revision, which a repair or a founding transfer moves on every record alike. Raw identity, not the state id, keeps an opened door, a connected fence or a lit furnace the building's own block instead of a divergence. The foundation row keeps the grant a new site already has: there the floor layer replaces the ground-surface block by construction, so a cell at the origin's own Y is admitted when the site search would admit that surface — floor-replaceable, no block entity, and no retained player edit.

**Everything else standing inside the village's territory is the village's own property (owner D27, 2026-11-18):** the plan clears that cell first and the debit credits its drop into the village's storage, whatever actor built it — block history is not consulted for the placement fence inside the territory. Such cells are planned as ordinary clear-stage work before the placement, so the drop is credited exactly once and no material is charged for it. **Outside** the territory a divergent cell still refuses admission with `PreviousBuildDiverged`, naming the cell in the player message, the owner's queue status and one `settlement_construction_order_refused` warning per distinct reason, before any receipt, debit or block write. A cell holding a **block entity** (a chest, furnace, barrel or other container) refuses in both cases: clearing it would destroy its contents, which no drop credit covers, and the executor would otherwise refuse `Blocked` after the project is paid for. A natural clearable cut whose block is not the building's own is refused outside the territory rather than credited automatically. An autonomous planner skips a building whose fence refuses it and tries the next funded one, so one diverged site cannot starve the other buildings' upgrades. `Cancelled` records are not evidence, committed records are never pruned, and the chain is read as committed at admission time, so a rewrite by recovery or `reopen_emptied` is honoured. Residual: with block history disabled, a player block on the foundation row **outside** the territory is indistinguishable from the site's ground, the same residual the site search carries.

**Residual of the fence's scope.** Only placement cells are fenced: a `removals` or `cuts` cell carries its own air target and erases whatever stands there (a natural plant, or a payload the debit credits by its drops) without comparing it against the committed evidence. That is §2.4's decision — the clear half keeps D18/F3's rules, whose own evidence, attribution and persistence checks apply — and it is not a rollover gap the fence was meant to close.

**Inside the territory (owner D27.4):** the N5 attribution rule above and the N6 executor identity check below apply outside the working settlement's territory. Inside it a tree cell is village property: no history, attribution, persistent-leaf mark or generator evidence is read, and the settlement's own registered planting still decides which whole trees are felled.

**Executor identity (N6):** the prepared executor no longer trusts its manifest for identity. Before a clear cell is debited, a **log** must still match deterministic regeneration or stand inside a registered root's own column and height bound; selection certifies exactly that set, because a tree log outside the root's column makes the whole component ambiguous and refuses the lot before any clearing; a **leaf** needs no such check because player placement always sets `persistent=true` and persistent leaves are refused. Missing forest evidence is a `Unloaded` wait rather than a refusal, and a registered root retires only through the ordinary retire pass at an empty trunk column, never cell by cell, so a tree's unfinished crown keeps its identity evidence until the last cell. The stall watch uses the same predicate, so a lost-evidence cell still reports `protected_tree_in_preparation` and takes the cancel/requeue recovery.

**Accepted risk (owner):** with block history disabled or expired, a player log placed exactly in, or attached to, a registered tree's trunk column is indistinguishable from that trunk and may be felled with conserved drops; outside the owning settlement's territory, non-tree blocks in a build cell are replaced regardless of history (only block entities are checked before the write). Block history is on by default (`core` and `mc-edge` configuration) with a 200 000 record / 30 day retention, so the residual needs capture deliberately off or an expired record. This residual is not proof of player authorship, and it never covers a log the retained history attributes to a player. Terrain edits share it: a player-placed natural block in the work volume is protected only while its block-history attribution is retained (30 days or 200 000 records); after expiry it is ordinary natural ground again. Inside territory, history is not an authority for village-owned cells (D27, below).

Site checks, ground discovery, preparation, loot credit and the stall watch share the permission; admission changes neither blocks nor planting metadata. Clearing a registered root's last trunk cell empties its column; the ordinary forest retire pass then removes the registration in a journaled revision bump, so the inventory credit and the world edits of that tree are never split by a retirement in the middle. **Inside the territory a registered planted tree on a street column the building's walking corridor needs is felled whole, like a tree on the lot (owner D7, 2026-11-17; D44):** the construction forecast walks through its crown, the preparation clears the trunk and head-height cells (up to two blocks above the ground) and marks the root so the whole tree goes, and its drops enter real storage. A crown above that headroom is no obstacle until then. Beyond the territory, outside-lot access columns keep their future-crown protection unless they are part of the selected whole tree. A missing access route cannot bypass protection merely because the lot is empty. A winding walk's rectangular executor envelope may enclose unrelated trees; that empty space is not clearance work and does not veto admission.

**Provenance lifetime outside the territory (PrepLifecycle §2.3, owner D27/D28).** A manifest records the block-log boundary its survey ran under. Outside the owning settlement's territory sphere (a founding site, or cells beyond the sphere's edge), a block that a commit destroys (a dig, a plant cut, or a placement over a block) is refused while it carries a retained player edit. Its survey must also still be covered: the log has to retain every attributed edit made since the survey. When eviction or a capture-off restart has broken that coverage, the remaining plan is admitted again as a fresh survey would admit it. If no remaining destructive cell outside the territory carries a retained player edit, work continues under a fresh admission (while capture is off, the stored admission is kept). Otherwise the commit is refused as `Blocked`, before any cut or credit, and no pause is stored. The ordinary stall path then cancels and requeues unpaid work, and paid work waits until that player edit leaves the log. With time eviction off (`[block_log] retention_days = 0`), that wait can be endless: this is the one exception to "never endless", and it is not silent. After the episode's first stall report, the owner is told the blocking cell once per episode (`<blueprint> is waiting: a player's block at x y z outside the village is in the way.`). The notice counts only once the owner's queue has accepted it. An owner who is offline when the episode is reported, or has chat hidden, still gets the notice after logging in or showing chat again, even with no new progress. So does an owner whose stall turns into such a refusal later. **Accepted residual (Main, 2026-11-18), identical identity only:** like any fresh survey, a re-admission cannot see a player edit the log has already evicted. As a result, an evicted edit whose block still has the surveyed identity (for example sand replaced by sand) is not protected. A cell whose identity differs from its survey is refused while coverage has lapsed. Inside the territory block history is not consulted. A manifest surveyed with capture off keeps the residual above.

Preparation uses the existing ledger stages in disposition order: `liquid` → `saplings` → `trees` → `cut` → `fill` → `build`; empty portions are omitted and derived `repair` work is last. The leading liquid portion contains source fills; their original material bill is retained, not duplicated. The ledger persists each portion's ordered raw-block expected-before identities and registered roots (read when detecting a sapling grown since admission). Certificates simulate earlier portions at overlapping positions. Every still-unprocessed cell is checked immediately before work, including after a restart or midway through a portion; already-completed cells are never billed again, and their original evidence is not rewritten.

Inside the owning settlement's three-dimensional territory (owner D27), a changed cell is re-derived from the live world, not paused for stale admission evidence. Its actual destroyed block's canonical loot enters physical storage in the same transaction as placement payment, block writes and cursor advancement. Already-air clear cells credit nothing; full storage, non-empty containers and fluid-safety refusals publish no partial custody. Preparation/removal drops are rolled with the canonical suitable tool: the lowest ordinary tier allowed by settlements' implicit extraction-tool authority, without silk touch or fortune; known zero-drop blocks such as glass may yield nothing. Unchanged foundation placement keeps its original bill and does not earn new ground income or perform air-cut fluid checks. History is not an authority for village-owned cells. Outside territory, a changed cell requires continuous provenance coverage as well as passing per-cell forestry/history vetoes; lapsed coverage or a veto returns `Blocked` without storing a stale pause, while covered, unprotected natural drift proceeds on the same project. A registered sapling grown to a tree before its first cut takes the existing unpaid cancel/requeue path so selection certifies the whole grown tree.

Before refreshing a lapsed admission, recheck every still-unprocessed outside-territory coordinate, including later portions. Its first unprocessed occurrence compares the certificate with the live world; later occurrences compare with the preceding planned result at that coordinate, so a cut-to-air followed by fill is not a false refusal. Completed cells are excluded. An unchanged current portion must not renew coverage for a later player edit whose history record was evicted; refusal leaves the admission, world, inventory and cursor untouched and uses the ordinary once-per-episode owner notice naming the refused cell.

Non-terrain blocks under a placement are credited exactly once, changed or unchanged. The planner's ground class (`excavatable`: soil, stone, sand, gravel and clay, plus managed village soil) stays uncredited under placement even after a foreign change, so foundations do not manufacture stock. Air-target cuts retain their own single credit. Waterlogged blocks use their ordinary canonical drops; only actual water/lava blocks are liquid-fill work. Drop recipients must survive the step: a container being replaced, including either half of a paired chest, cannot receive that step's credit; without other fitting physical storage the step refuses before payment or publication.

Canonical demolition supplies the acting Builder to loot evaluation. Empty `this`-entity predicates therefore use real actor presence; unsupported non-empty entity predicates remain unavailable rather than silently dropping their conditions. Snow and layered snow use those same vanilla tables and the ordinary implicit tool, not a block-name fallback.

Recovery scans completed `liquid`, `fill` and `build` cells for repair. Free soil retirement neither repairs free ground nor hides an earlier paid floor at the same coordinate. Every repair/truncate/reopen changes stage plans, cell batches and certificates together through the structure ledger's marked per-record write API. Settlement admission images are retained, not recomputed over the whole box; generic plugin structures retain their exact whole-box fencing.

Registered saplings on the lot form the `saplings` portion, ahead of `trees`. A `footprint_changed` pause cancels and requeues an unpaid preparation only for its own stale-tree hazard: the current portion is `saplings`, no cell has been felled yet, the order keeps its queue slot (no newer pending order would take it), and a registered root in the remaining manifest is no longer a sapling. Natural leaf decay, a foreign edit, any started felling and any later stage re-snapshot the footprint and resume the same structure; a newer order never takes the active order's slot. Fresh selection must certify any regrown tree before further work; paid construction retains its existing recovery behavior.

Before replanting, lumberjacks preview the same loaded street and housing geometry with the proposed future crown excluded. A planting may change the preferred route or lot, but must retain access to already reachable standing buildings and a remaining feasible lot for each previously feasible housing blueprint, independently of current stock. The preview first excludes settlements whose territory and outpost claims are horizontally disjoint from the crown; a distant town's cold cache cannot veto local renewal. Unused theoretical streets are not a blanket planting ban: a proved remaining house lot and access suffice even if unrelated forest evidence is pending elsewhere. Unknown evidence still refuses a planting when no remaining lot can be proved. The preview changes no world blocks, stock or durable reservations, and never removes an existing planting or weakens managed-tree protection.

Builders approach a tree's footing within four blocks and clear the selected tree cells through the canonical one-cell executor, including the rest of a needed registered tree outside the lot and walking corridor. A preparation cell is a forestry-planned tree cell, including one already emptied by leaf decay or a player; it is cleared from a dry, open stand within four blocks of that cell's footing. A stand is a cell the builder's body can stand on, so the top of a fence or wall post never counts. On uneven ground the builder walks to such a stand rather than the nearest one, and a cell with no such stand is cleared from the perimeter like any blueprint cell, so it never holds the project slot. Forced leaf removal uses vanilla loot without a tool (saplings, sticks and apples by chance); those drops and log drops are credited atomically to real settlement storage before removal, and full storage leaves the block untouched. Surface plants a new building or street clears also yield their vanilla loot without a tool, so short grass drops wheat seeds one time in eight. That loot goes to storage; what storage cannot hold drops at the cleared cell. It is how a settlement gets the seeds for its first farm. Unselected leaves are left to vanilla decay. Once preparation is complete, normal footprint/perimeter construction rules apply.

Ordinary grass and other non-tree clear cells use the perimeter rule and its twelve-block work reach (infrastructure such as meadows can be worked while walked on); they do not send a meadow Builder toward an unnecessary felling stand. An already-air clear cell still uses the column-footing rule, so a planned tree cell cannot lose its target when it decays.

### Perimeter, gates and well

From 10 residents (the threshold the single wall section used to have) the planner walls the settlement in and gives it a well. The wall is not a blueprint building: it is street-style work taken from the plan with the project slot, bought from real stock and built by Builders one project at a time, in this order:

- **Well.** A 3 × 3 cobblestone rim, one block high, around one water source set into the ground, on the first plaza corner that is level (within one block) and free of buildings and containers: north-west, then north-east, then south-west (the south-east corner holds the crossing's lamp). The rim costs 8 cobblestone; the water source is free, because no storage holds a filled bucket. A well standing on any corner ends the work.
- **Perimeter.** The square line 30 blocks from the bell on both axes (240 columns), the outer verge of the first ring road, cut into projects of at most 11 columns: per side a gatehouse where the road through the bell crosses it, then the wall outward on both sides of the gatehouse, north, east, south, west. The other roads that cross the line get plain wall; the ring road inside connects them. A wall column is 3 blocks of cobblestone on the ground; a gatehouse is seven columns 4 blocks tall with one oak door in the road's centre column (the door's upper half is free), so the road stays open through a door. Wall and gate columns follow the ground; a column on water, a cliff, a tree, a building or outside the territory is left out and stays a gap (terrain-blocked stays blocked). The whole ring costs 748 cobblestone and 4 oak doors.
- **Nothing is stored.** What stands is read from the world on every pass: finished columns (stacked cobblestone, a door) and a standing well owe nothing, and the lazy search stops at the first project the stock can fund. Each stretch, gatehouse and well is built once: a finished one stays reserved in the structure ledger, so a column lost later is not rebuilt by the planner. An unfunded project does not stop a funded later one, and none starts while an extraction worker is out for a priority bill or while another project is running. Builders stand inside the wall (or on the road beside the well's rim) to place a column.
- **Roads and meadows.** The fortification is planned before street work (streets reach every standing building in the radius and owe work without end); the meadow clearing for a first Farm's seeds still goes first. Finished road paving and meadow clearing hold nothing the wall must protect, so a stretch may cross a paved road. A road stretch whose area the wall or a gatehouse already holds is not paved later and stays a gap; lots and other clearings honour both.

The wall columns are the plan's `Wall` cells: no lot or lamp post stands on them and no frontage forms across them, so buildings stand inside the wall (frontages facing outward from the ring road are unused; the ring road's lamp columns on the line get no post), and the road network beyond a built gatehouse is no longer walked.

## Builders and supplies

An eligible adult resident can become a Builder; the planner prefers unemployed residents and may borrow eligible miners or lumberjacks, restoring their profession afterwards. Farmers, shepherds, soldiers, nitwits and traded villagers are not recruited, and miners and lumberjacks are lent only while the settlement has a single project. Autonomous work therefore opens a new site only when every active project has its Builder and some resident could take one more: a free native Builder, or a resident this rule recruits at the project count the new site makes. A resident whose last route to storage failed in the past 1200 ticks does not count; the planner itself searches no route, and this memory is runtime only. An owner's order is admitted on its own path whether or not a Builder is free. A Builder who cannot reach storage can be released for another candidate. A Builder whose project has not advanced a cell in 3000 shift ticks (a hostile mob that never closes in can keep a resident fleeing and returning on the only road to the lot) is released the same way; the replaced Builder is chosen last for the next 24,000 ticks. The watch is runtime only and is not saved. Work requires the daytime work shift, ticks 2000–8999, and `mob_griefing` enabled.

With no failure on record the gate may still open a site that nobody can reach from storage. If the binding pass then tries residents for it and every route to storage fails, the autonomous site that pass opened is withdrawn unbuilt through the ordinary cancellation (`settlement_site_withdrawn`), so it never holds the planner; the failures it recorded keep the gate closed until they lapse. A resident whose failure still stands is not tried for any project until it lapses. An owner's order is never withdrawn this way.

Construction enumerates living settlement members through the same derived occupancy index used for population counts, not through the civil radius. Builders remain eligible while travelling to and working at a daughter outpost outside that radius; their carried materials remain visible to the active bill. This membership index is rebuilt from native entity columns after recovery and is never a persisted roster.

After three in-game work hours without a completed cell, the runtime watch emits one WARN event, `settlement_construction_stalled`, with the settlement, blueprint, origin, stage, work counter, next cell, and a named `cause`. Builder rotations do not repeat the event until real progress starts a new episode. Paused work is observed too. If a protected tree blocks unpaid clearing, the old structure is cancelled without removing the tree or changing paid stock; its owner order is requeued when no newer pending order exists. This protection includes player-edited trees outside the territory and managed trees outside the lot, not the clearable managed cells on the needed lot. Paid construction is never discarded.

Already-grown protected crowns may leave no alternative site; an order refused for `PlantingProvenance` remains pending.

Village paths and farmland on a needed lot are removed, and the road network stays connected: a path the lot would cut is re-laid around it ([owner decision](OWNER_DECISIONS.md#lots-and-terrain-inside-the-settlement--owner-2026-11-17)). Parallel projects share that proof (D8, P2-5): an unfinished structure holds its whole detour corridor against a neighbour lot, and every plan mounts the active projects' manifests — their unbuilt detour stands as nodes, their unretired village line is already gone — so two individually valid plans cannot cut the network together. A free pavement cell is refused when the ground under it is gone (a player-dug column), never laid as a block from nothing. The network is read from the world on every plan, never stored: its nodes are the plan's road, plaza and verge columns plus every walkable dirt-path column, joined on foot by steps of at most one block from the bell. A lot may retire only path cells whose loss leaves every remaining node and every door connected to the bell; a piece the lot cuts off is bridged by a detour of new path cells outside the lot and its one-block clearance. The detour paves natural soil for free, exactly as street paving does, and any fill under a raised detour cell is real storage dirt; it never touches a player-placed block (`ProtectedObstruction`) or a fluid, and it may not cross a wall column or a certified building. When no detour exists the lot is refused with `NoPathDetour`. The detour stands before any old path cell is retired, so the walk is connected at every executor tick; the retired path and farmland cells become the soil they are made of (a dirt path and farmland are dirt), at no material cost and with no drop, and the lot's crops — wheat, carrots, potatoes, beetroots and the flower crops — leave as removal cells whose vanilla harvest the clear stage credits to settlement storage, exactly as the settlement's own farmers reap one (a ripe plant gives its produce and one seed, an unripe one gives the seed back); storage that cannot hold the harvest spills it at the cell, as vanilla does. Farmland, a path or a crop inside another building's certified footprint stays refused by the ordinary one-block building buffer (`BuildingBuffer`). Isolated path remnants proved disconnected from the bell are retired without a detour.

Material stalls name the next item and quantity, carried quantity, canonical pickup point, the incumbent's UUID, position and navigation state, and its last executor refusal (including failed fetches). `recovery` reports the action actually taken, such as `builder_released`, rather than only the optional protected-tree preparation retry; the separate `preparation` field retains that retry's result. An ordinary builder rotation preserves the paid project, pending order and real stock. Nights do not count toward the 3000-shift-tick watch.

A released Builder's load of its project's materials, including the later stages' materials one trip carries, becomes a recorded return duty before another Builder takes the project; a later pass delivers it to storage. The load a Builder still carries once every cell of its project is placed is returned the same way.

If the first recovery leaves the same cell and work counter unchanged for another 3000 shift ticks, `settlement_construction_recovery_failed` names the project, cell, `first_recovery` outcome, current cause and current recovery. Outcomes such as `no_builder` or `project_paused` are not actions. It reports once per unchanged-progress episode, not once per builder rotation. Unpaid work (nothing consumed yet, such as free road paving or a project that never placed a cell) gives back its slot at this point the same way as at a protected tree, with recovery `preparation_cancelled`: placed cells stay in the world, carried material returns to storage, and an owner order is requeued when no newer pending order exists. A rotation that only picks Builders who cannot reach the worksite (residents trapped in a one-cell water basin under a tree crown; a wall stretch whose inward stand columns are open water) would otherwise hold the only slot forever. Paid work continues to retry without being discarded or overtaken by a later order.

A funded pending order with no site emits `settlement_construction_site_refused` after its bounded cursor has examined at least one full street-frontage scan. The WARN names the settlement and blueprint, frontage and examined counts, rejection checks and one candidate position per check (including protected obstructions, existing buildings, terrain and the prepared material bill). Unchanged refusal checks do not repeat the event on later scans; a changed set of checks or a new order starts a new report. This watch is runtime-only, and neither logging nor a blocked order relaxes player protection, outside-lot tree protection or FIFO admission.

Construction admissions select a settlement every four ticks. One admission performs either a real material transfer or one cell of construction, not both. The Builder batches materials into its real carry slots and walks to the site. For blueprint work, its entire body must be outside the full future footprint and the next cell within **12 blocks** of its feet; perimeter targets are two cells outside the footprint. While walking around terrain, the Builder retains a supported committed perimeter target while it still reaches the next cell; a failed route or an invalid stand is selected afresh. Repeatedly choosing the nearest adjacent stand can otherwise reverse the route at every step of a hillside detour. This recovery keeps the same paid project, placed blocks and physical carry; it does not cancel or refund construction. Missing materials wait for replenishment. Completion checks the resulting certificate.

If an active project's remaining bill loses its funding, the planner staffs the missing extraction job. When its only suitable worker is the project's borrowed Builder, it releases that worker back to extraction until real deliveries fund the remaining bill; the project stays in place. A lumberjack gathering for that shortfall seeks the project's fixed wood family. A partial load that funds the remaining bill is returned to storage rather than held until a stack fills.

A partial lumber load triggers an early delivery only when it closes a funding gap. If the candidate or active remaining bill is already funded, normal harvesting continues instead of sending each newly cut log home while the planner waits for a legal site.

The planner tries wood species by available stock and chooses one that can fund the complete bill, including oak as a candidate. The chosen species and bed color are fixed in the admitted project's cells. A bed needs three wool of the same color; local eligible workers can shear an adult sheep using real shears, including shears crafted from two iron ingots. Construction iron demand can be supplied by workers carrying real ore and fuel to an owned ordinary furnace and returning ingots to storage. Unemployed residents, shepherds and miners load the furnace; other employed residents only carry its output to storage. The demand is the larger of the active project's remaining bill and the largest skipped autonomous candidate, so a funded later building does not stop the shears for an unfunded house bed. When needed and fundable, a Builder can place a furnace near storage using a stored furnace or one crafted from eight cobblestone. These supplies are not free.

Builders can use bounded recursive vanilla shaped and shapeless crafting chains, such as logs to planks to doors or planks and wool to beds. Fetch and crafting commit against real storage and carry; insufficient inputs or carry space do not partially debit the batch. This crafting path does not itself smelt or stonecut. Farmland is free (the soil already in place is hoed, as paving is shoveled; the Farm bills no dirt), wheat costs seeds, door upper halves and bed heads are free counterpart cells, and double slabs cost two slabs. Water is not created for fields.

The whole-bill proof and the Builder's hauling account for wood needed by the remaining species-specific cells. Generic recipes use stock left after that reservation, so a mixed-species stock can fund generic items without exhausting the project's chosen wood. Crafting yields whole batches, and surplus from a batch remains available for later cells.

The content registry rebuilds an immutable, ordered recipe/ingredient index with every recipe or tag installation. Boolean funding can reject a bill whose ingredient dependency has no available base stock; cycles, the depth boundary and custom-model inputs or outputs remain conservative and fall through to the ordinary proof. Quantities, physical slot choice, wood reservations, whole-batch surplus, capacity and work limits still use that proof, with per-proof derived counts and an exact slot undo journal.

A non-persisted settlement-registry memo retains at most 16 Boolean or wood-reservation proofs together. Its keys compare the probe kind, full ordered bill, every slot and stack component by value, and a strongly owned recipe-index identity; it is never saved, so a loaded world starts without it. Native callers run on the tick owner, not entity workers. Concurrent misses may duplicate computation and vary eviction order, never results. The typed structure ledger is likewise derived from the canonical plugin domain: every write, mutable borrow, deletion and WAL replay of any key of that domain invalidates it. The cache itself is never saved; the checkpoint and journal carry the domain's per-record keys ([layout](../architecture/persistence.md#structure-ledger-keys)).

A village construction edit follows the per-cell property and outside-territory coverage rules above, without a whole-footprint stale pause. The unpaid grown-sapling case alone pauses as `footprint_changed` for its owner order to cancel and requeue. A solid foreign block in an already placed cell stays and keeps the geometric certificate. A placed authored cell that an edit emptied (air or a replaceable plant where the blueprint needs a block, for example a player breaking a placed log) is re-placed: the bounded completion pass queues emptied paid cells into a final `repair` stage of the same canonical structure, and a second break requeues that one stage. The Builder uses the ordinary loop and pays the cell's material again because the edit destroyed the block. Explicit owner pauses are not automatically resumed. The project certifies only when the pass finds nothing emptied. Roads, meadows, lamps and the furnace workstation have no certificate and are not rechecked. Removing a settlement cancels its construction handle but leaves placed blocks and carried materials.

## Owner build requests

Chat build commands target the claimed settlement containing the player and require its owner or an operator:

- `/settlement build <blueprint>` requests a new level-one blueprint.
- `/settlement build upgrade <buildingId>` requests an available blueprint upgrade by building ID; it is not the same as rechecking a certificate.
- `/settlement build cancel` cancels the first pending owner order, not active work. `/settlement build cancel <n>` cancels the n-th pending order (1-based); an absent index reports `No pending construction order #<n>.` The Projects status screen lists every pending order and its own waiting reason, so the queue can be inspected and trimmed without draining it.

The **Projects** menu offers `Order: <blueprint>`, available upgrade buttons, **Status**, and **Cancel pending order**. Menu targeting can select a nearby settlement or an owned-settlement fallback; permission is checked on the selected claimed settlement, without requiring the player to stand inside it. See [menus](menus.md).

Project and pending-order labels use the blueprint's authored ID when no localized name exists, so cultural and custom blueprints remain distinguishable instead of all appearing as “Building”.

An order can start when fewer than **eight** projects are active and the full real-material bill, including crafting and site preparation, can be funded **on top of every other active project** — the admission proof is joint, so two projects that are each fundable alone are not both admitted out of the same stock. Site preparation material (its own foundation dirt) is part of the admitted project's remaining bill and waits for real delivery instead of blocking admission. Admission itself neither places blocks nor debits stock, and one planning pass admits at most one project. Active work is never preempted by a later request, including untouched autonomous projects. Each project builds at its own pace: several projects on one settlement advance in the same tick, each with its own builder, its own bill and its own stall watch, so a stuck site never masks another. A project's builder is the resident bound to it, and only that resident advances it; a builder that dies, loses its profession or walks away is replaced, and any materials it carried go back to that project's account. While pending, an unfunded order blocks unrelated new autonomous projects; current work, its own furnace prerequisite and existing founding handover/cancellation can proceed. Refused requests are not queued, and the ninth pending request is refused with `Construction queue is full: MAX_ORDERS=8.` **Projects** and **Status** report progress and unmet needs per project; cancelling a pending order releases autonomous planning.

A queued upgrade retains the exact level confirmed by the owner. The next request follows the highest completed, active or queued level for that building. If a prerequisite upgrade is cancelled, a later level stays pending with a reason naming the next required level: it never starts the cancelled level or silently changes its own level or material bill. Cancel the dependent orders and request the desired chain again to change it. If the target building disappears or the stored level has no blueprint definition, the planner drops the invalid order and notifies the online owner. A wall order builds one blueprint wall section with Builders and a gate order one blueprint gate; neither is certification of player blocks, and neither is part of the automatic [perimeter](#perimeter-gates-and-well), whose wall and gatehouses are plan work, not certified buildings.

## Certification commands

Certification inspects loaded blocks without placing them or charging materials. Certify and list select the settlement containing the player, choosing the nearest anchor and then lowest ID if radii overlap. Certification requires the owner or an operator of a claimed settlement; certificate upgrades address a building globally and require management permission for its settlement.

- `/settlement building certify <kind> <x1> <y1> <z1> <x2> <y2> <z2>` certifies level 1 using inclusive, ordered minimum and maximum coordinates. Include floor and roof where required.
- `/settlement building upgrade <id>` checks the existing bounds for the next certificate level, up to 3; build the extra blocks first. This does not order Builder work.
- `/settlement building list` shows IDs, kinds, levels and bounds.

Re-certifying the same kind with bounds containing an existing certificate refreshes or expands it without changing its ID or level. Other overlapping certificates are rejected. Bounds must fit inside the settlement and loaded chunks, with at most 64 blocks per axis and 32,768 blocks in total. A settlement can have at most 1,024 buildings.

Kinds are `house`, `storage`, `barracks`, `wall`, `tower`, `gate`, `mine`, `lumber_camp` and profession workshops: `armorer`, `butcher`, `cartographer`, `cleric`, `farmer`, `fisherman`, `fletcher`, `leatherworker`, `librarian`, `mason`, `builder`, `shepherd`, `toolsmith`, `weaponsmith`.

## Certificate requirements

| Requirement | Level 1 | Level 2 | Level 3 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Footprint area, except wall | 9 | 16 | 25 |
| Inclusive height, except wall and tower | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| House beds | 1 | 2 | 3 |
| Barracks beds | 2 | 4 | 6 |
| Storage containers | 1 | 2 | 3 |
| Workshop, mine or camp workstations and job slots | 1 | 2 | 3 |
| Wall length × height | 5 × 3 | 9 × 4 | 13 × 5 |
| Tower height | 6 | 8 | 10 |
| Gate doors | 1 | 2 | 3 |

Ordinary buildings need a collision-bearing floor across the selection; a mine's bounds instead designate a digging zone. Houses and barracks need correctly paired beds, a door, a complete roof on the top plane and a closed collision-bearing perimeter between floor and roof. Towers need the roof and perimeter but not beds or a door. Walls need a continuous collision-bearing vertical plane along the longer axis at the minimum perpendicular coordinate; gates need four solid corner columns as well as doors. These are geometric checks, not a style requirement. Beds for breeding have their own rules; see [breeding](breeding.md).

Workshops, mines and camps use distinct matching work points in their bounds, with the first 1/2/3 in coordinate order providing slots. Destroying a workstation releases its slot; unloaded chunks alone do not. A workstation moved within the same building keeps the slot: when a jobless resident looks for work, a work point that lost its workstation moves to an unlisted matching one in the bounds (any vanilla job block or barrel; a moved crafting table needs re-certification). Storage recognizes chests, trapped chests and barrels, up to 64 registered points. Barracks provide two soldier slots per level; see [army](army.md). For workstation types and worker behavior, see [jobs](jobs.md).

## Natural zones and radius

Generated-village adoption can certify a natural level-one mine or lumber zone without a placed workstation or chest. It has one open entrance and one job slot. A lumber zone needs at least four logs; a mine needs at least 32 stone or ore blocks, a 5 × 5 horizontal area and two blocks of depth below its entrance. Bounds must be loaded, within the radius and nonoverlapping. Workers extract the actual finite resources; upgrading requires the ordinary geometry and workstation.

Natural-zone discovery probes outward over the saved radius, at most four candidates per 20-tick pass, using loaded terrain and avoiding generated-piece horizontal footprints. Founding mine candidates use a 15 × 15 footprint, with an entrance on the west edge near a Z edge; quarry depth follows the supported entrance height. The 5 × 5 certification minimum does not guarantee sufficient supplies.

The creation radius is a floor. Population growth raises the radius to at least four blocks per resident, capped at 256; it never shrinks. For example, 30 residents require radius 120. `/settlement info` displays it.
